Hospitality Furniture Sourcing Checklist: What to Confirm Before You Order

Most hospitality sourcing problems trace back to something that wasn't confirmed explicitly before production started. This checklist covers the specific points worth locking down before any order moves forward.

Specification and grade

  • Contract-grade foam density and fabric rub-count confirmed, not left to factory default
  • Fire safety certification requirement stated explicitly for your destination market
  • Finish and colour approved against a physical sample, not a digital rendering

Production and capacity

  • Factory has verifiable prior experience with hospitality-scale, contract-grade orders
  • Production capacity confirmed against your order volume and your required delivery window
  • Subcontracting disclosed upfront if any part of the order is produced by a second workshop

Inspection and shipping

  • Three-stage inspection scheduled: pre-production, mid-line, pre-shipment
  • Consolidation plan confirmed if the order spans multiple factories or categories
  • Freight and customs timeline built backward from your fixed opening or handover date, with buffer

Commercial terms

These are the terms that decide what happens when something goes wrong, so they are worth agreeing in writing rather than assuming.

  • Payment schedule confirmed against production milestones, not calendar dates — deposit, balance before shipment, and what triggers each
  • Price validity period stated, plus how raw material or currency movement is handled if production runs long
  • Rework and claims process defined: who pays for rework, who bears return freight, and by when a claim must be raised

Documentation

A hospitality order spans several categories and often several factories, which makes the document set the likeliest place for a mismatch.

  • Commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading reconciled before the container sails, with HS codes stated per line
  • Certificate of origin arranged where a preferential duty rate applies to your destination market
  • Test reports or certification for flammability, foam density or any regulated material held on file and available to your broker

Risk and contingency

Hospitality projects fail on timelines more often than on quality, and the contingency for a slip is best agreed before it is needed.

  • Schedule buffer agreed explicitly, and the point at which a delay becomes your risk rather than the supplier's
  • Marine insurance in place and the claims route understood, including who inspects damage on arrival
  • Spare and replacement stock decided upfront — quantities to hold back, and where they will be stored
