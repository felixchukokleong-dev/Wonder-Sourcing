The three checkpoints

Every order is checked before, during and after production.

Each checkpoint exists to catch a different category of fault — materials issues can't be fixed once a piece is finished, and packing faults can't be seen until the container is loaded.

STAGE01

Pre-production inspection

Before cutting begins, we check that the wood, fabric, foam density, hardware and finish materials on hand match what your order specifies — the point where a wrong batch of fabric is cheapest to catch.

STAGE02

During-production (mid-line) inspection

With around 20–30% of the order complete, we check joint stability, seam quality, colour and finish consistency against your approved sample, and flag any drift early enough to correct it.

STAGE03

Pre-shipment inspection

Once the order is complete and packed, we run a full carton count against the packing list, a drop test on a sample carton, and a final visual check — before anything is authorised to load.

What you receive

A report you can act on, not a pass/fail stamp.

  • Photo & video evidence. Timestamped images of the checked items, not a generic sample photo.
  • AQL-based sampling. Carton sampling follows standard AQL levels appropriate to your order size.
  • Same-day turnaround. Reports go out the day the inspection is completed, so a hold decision doesn't cost you transit time.
  • A clear call. Ship, rework, or reject — stated plainly, with the reasoning behind it.
If something fails

What happens when an inspection doesn't pass.

STEPHold

The shipment is held

Nothing that fails pre-shipment inspection is authorised to load. We notify you the same day with photo evidence of the fault.

STEPRework

The factory reworks at their cost

Where the fault is fixable — a finish issue, a packing error — the factory corrects it at their own expense and we re-inspect before release.

STEPEscalate

Unresolved issues are escalated to you

If a factory can't or won't correct a fault, we bring it back to you with options — replacement production, a partial shipment, or a different supplier — before anything moves further.

Questions

Common questions about furniture inspection.

What is furniture quality control in China and why does it matter?

Quality control is the independent check on an order before it ships. A factory grading its own work is the single most common reason containers arrive with damage or mismatched finishes. We run three checkpoints - pre-production, mid-line and pre-shipment - so each stage catches a different category of fault before it is too late to fix.

What are the three stages of furniture inspection?

Pre-production, before cutting begins, where we check that the wood, fabric, foam density, hardware and finish materials on hand match your specification. Mid-line, at around 20-30% of the order complete, checking joint stability, seam quality and colour consistency. Pre-shipment, once packed, with a full carton count against the packing list, a drop test and a loading check.

How long does a furniture inspection take to report?

Reports go out the same day the inspection is completed, so a hold decision does not cost you transit time. You receive timestamped photo and video evidence of the items checked, not a generic sample photo.

What happens if my furniture fails inspection?

The shipment is held - nothing that fails pre-shipment inspection is authorised to load, and you are notified the same day with photo evidence. Where the fault is fixable, such as a finish issue or packing error, the factory reworks at their own expense. If a factory cannot or will not correct it, we bring it back to you with options.

Do you take commission from factories?

No - 0% commission from factories. That is what makes the inspection call independent; we are not financially attached to whether a shipment passes.

Can you inspect an order I am sourcing directly?

Yes. You can bring us in for inspection only, with no need to route the whole order through us. That applies to any order, not just ones we sourced.

How is your quality control sampling decided?

Carton sampling follows standard AQL levels appropriate to your order size, so the level of checking scales with the volume you are buying rather than applying one fixed sample to every shipment.
