Good to know

Notes for buyers shipping to Philippines.

  • MICP is the main entry point for Luzon-bound cargo; buyers in Cebu or Davao should confirm onward domestic transfer timing.
  • Developer handover schedules tend to be tight — we build inspection and freight timing around your fit-out deadline.
Customs and duty

Clearance into Manila.

Duty on furniture into the Philippines follows the declared HS code per item, with VAT applied on top — the calculator pre-fills 12% as an example only. The Philippines is part of the ASEAN–China free trade area, so Chinese-origin goods can qualify for preferential rates with a valid Form E certificate of origin matching the commercial invoice and packing list.

For Luzon deliveries the ocean leg ends at Manila International Container Port. For Cebu, Davao or anywhere in the Visayas and Mindanao, the container then moves again domestically, and that second leg can add weeks rather than days. Treat the onward transfer as its own schedule with its own buffer, because it fails for different reasons than production does.

Timing

Building around a handover date.

Philippine orders are frequently tied to a developer handover or a lease commencement, which is a hard date with penalties attached. Working backwards from it, the binding constraint is rarely the factory — it is the sequence of inspection, loading, sailing and clearance, where each stage needs room to slip without consuming the next one’s margin.

The practical approach is to bring inspection forward and hold the balance payment until it passes, rather than trying to compress freight. Freight cannot be compressed by paying more; a defect found before loading can still be corrected, and the same defect found after arrival cannot.

Watch out for

Three things that catch Philippines buyers.

  • Tight handover schedules. Developer timelines rarely leave slack for a rework loop. If the specification is not locked before production starts, the schedule absorbs the correction.
  • Underestimating the domestic leg. Cargo bound beyond Luzon moves twice. Buyers who budget only the ocean transit are usually surprised by the second one.
  • Manila congestion at peak. Berth and yard pressure at MICP follows its own seasonal pattern. Arrival windows are worth planning with your broker rather than assuming.
