Good to know

Notes for buyers shipping to Malaysia.

Customs and duty

Clearance into Port Klang.

Duty on furniture into Malaysia follows the declared HS code per item, and sales tax applies — the calculator pre-fills 10% SST as an example only. Malaysia’s SST is a sales tax rather than a VAT, so it does not work identically to the regimes in Vietnam, Thailand or Indonesia. Confirm how it applies to your goods and your own registration status with your broker.

Malaysia is part of the ASEAN–China free trade area, so Chinese-origin goods can qualify for preferential rates with a valid Form E certificate of origin, kept consistent with the invoice and packing list.

Distribution

Where your cargo actually needs to end up.

Port Klang is Malaysia’s busiest container port with frequent direct sailings from South China, which is why most shipments land there. But landing and arriving are different things: buyers in Penang, Johor Bahru or East Malaysia usually clear at Port Klang and then transfer domestically, and that leg belongs in the schedule from the start.

If you are furnishing across several states, decide early whether to ship to one entry point and distribute locally, or to split the order. Splitting can reduce inland cost, but it multiplies the clearance work and the number of places a delay can occur.

Watch out for

Three things that catch Malaysia buyers.

  • Inland transfer after Port Klang. Deliveries to Penang, Johor or East Malaysia move again after clearing, on a separate schedule from the ocean leg.
  • Treating SST like a VAT. It is a sales tax with its own rules about who is liable and when. Assuming it behaves like the VAT regimes elsewhere in the region produces wrong landed-cost figures.
  • Broad category ranges in one container. Mixed seating, casegoods and outdoor lines each classify separately. The more categories in one shipment, the more classification detail your broker needs up front.
