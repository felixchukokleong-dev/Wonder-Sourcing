Foshan vs Guangzhou vs Shenzhen: Where to Source Furniture in China

Search "furniture manufacturer China" and Guangzhou and Shenzhen dominate the results, mostly because that's where the trading offices and export agents are based. The factories themselves are concentrated somewhere else entirely.

Guangzhou: trade shows and trading companies

Guangzhou hosts the China International Furniture Fair and a large number of trading companies that represent factories from across the region. It's a useful place to see a wide range of products in one trip, but pricing usually includes a trading company margin on top of the factory's actual cost.

Shenzhen: design-led and export-focused, but not furniture's home base

Shenzhen's manufacturing strength is electronics and design-forward consumer goods. Furniture factories exist there, but in far smaller concentration than in Foshan — buyers searching Shenzhen for furniture are usually redirected to Foshan-based suppliers anyway.

Foshan: where the factories actually are

Shunde and Lecong, both districts of Foshan, hold the largest concentration of furniture factories and wholesale markets in the world. Buying here means visiting the factory floor directly rather than through an intermediary showroom, which usually means better pricing and faster answers on production questions.

Which one should you choose?

If you want to browse broadly and don't mind paying a trading margin, Guangzhou's fair season is a reasonable starting point. If you're placing a real order and want factory-direct pricing and control over quality, Foshan is where the actual sourcing work happens.

What each city costs you in time and money

The cities are close enough that a trip can combine them — Guangzhou and Foshan share a metro connection, and Shenzhen is an hour further — but treating them as one destination is how buyers lose days. Travel between districts within Foshan alone can consume an hour or more, and a schedule that assumes three factory visits and a showroom in one afternoon will not survive contact with the traffic.

The practical cost of choosing the wrong base is a misdirected trip: a week of hotel and flights spent in showrooms when the decision needed a factory floor, or the reverse. Deciding what the trip is actually for — browsing versus placing a specific order — should determine the itinerary before it is booked, not after.

Where the skills and the labour actually are

Furniture production is not one trade. Upholstery, solid-wood joinery, carving, lacquer finishing, metal fabrication and glass are different skills, and they concentrate in different places. Shunde's upholstery and casegoods lines draw on a labour pool that has been doing that work for decades; the same factory relocated to a city without that pool would struggle to hire at all.

This is why "could this be made somewhere cheaper" usually has a narrow answer. A factory's capability is partly its machines and largely its people, and the people are where the industry already is. Longjiang in particular hosts the materials market — fabric, foam, hardware, veneer — which is why lead times inside the cluster are shorter than they look.

The supply chain around the factory matters as much as the factory

A furniture factory is an assembly point for materials made by other specialists. Being inside the cluster means a fabric can be swapped, a foam density changed, or a replacement hinge sourced in a morning rather than a fortnight — and it means a factory can take on a specification change mid-production without derailing the schedule.

Move the same order to a factory outside the cluster and nothing about the furniture changes, but the responsiveness does. That is the practical reason buyers end up in Foshan rather than somewhere with lower labour costs: the advantage is not the factory alone, it is everything within an hour of it.
