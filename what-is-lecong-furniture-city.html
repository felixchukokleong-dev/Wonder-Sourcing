What Is Lecong Furniture City? Inside the World's Largest Furniture Market

Lecong Furniture City sits inside Foshan's Shunde district and is widely considered the largest furniture trading hub on earth by floor area — a sprawling complex of showrooms that would take days to walk in full.

What's actually inside

Lecong isn't a single building but a district-scale complex of wholesale malls, each specializing in different categories — solid wood dining and bedroom sets, office furniture, mattresses, and increasingly lighting and home decor sold alongside furniture lines.

Showroom prices are not factory prices

A common mistake is treating Lecong showroom prices as final. Most showrooms represent a specific factory or a small group of factories, and the price quoted on the floor is a starting point for negotiation once you know your real order volume.

It's open year-round, not just during trade fairs

Unlike Guangzhou's fair, which runs on a fixed calendar, Lecong's showrooms operate year-round. That makes it easier to plan a buying trip around your own schedule rather than a fixed exhibition window.

How to get the most out of a visit

Buyers who get the most value go in with a specific product brief, a target price already researched, and — ideally — a local contact who can help distinguish an export-ready factory from one that only sells domestically.

How the district is laid out — and why the route matters

Lecong is not a single market you can walk in an afternoon. It is kilometres of wholesale malls along a main road, and the malls group by category: one cluster leans towards solid wood dining and bedroom, another towards office and commercial furniture, another towards mattresses and bedding, with lighting and decor increasingly mixed through the newer buildings.

That geography is why a first visit without a plan tends to end in fatigue and a handful of business cards. Deciding which two or three categories you are actually buying this trip, and which malls carry them, is what turns days of walking into a shortlist.

Practical logistics for a first buying trip

Expect to cover a small number of showrooms properly rather than a large number superficially. Most buyers stay within the district or a short drive of it, and build in travel time because moving between buildings takes longer than the map suggests. Weekdays are quieter and worth more than weekends.

Also plan around the calendar rather than against it. Because the showrooms trade year-round, you are not tied to the Canton Fair windows — which is an advantage, since visiting away from fair season means more supplier attention and shorter waits for whoever is helping you.

Buying single pieces and samples, not just containers

Lecong is not only a container-volume market. Designers and smaller buyers regularly use it to source one-off pieces, samples or display items, and many showrooms will sell single units — usually at a noticeably higher unit price than volume pricing, and with the buyer arranging their own collection or courier.

That matters for feasibility work: a prototype or a client sample can often be bought and shipped by air far more cheaply than commissioning a factory run for a single item. What it will not give you is a matched set in quantity — for that, the order still has to go through production.
