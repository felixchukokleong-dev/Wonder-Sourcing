# Off-page / authority plan

## The finding that shapes everything

**Google Business Profile is not available in mainland China.** Google's own
supported-countries page states it plainly:

> "Business Profile is also not supported in China."
> Source: support.google.com/business/answer/6270107

So the standard advice — "set up a Google Business Profile" — cannot work for the
Foshan office, and no amount of effort changes that. Two things follow:

1. **Hong Kong IS supported by Google Business Profile**, and Wonder Sourcing
   Limited is a Hong Kong registered entity (BRN 77216103). A GBP tied to a
   verifiable Hong Kong address is a legitimate route to a Google presence.
2. **Bing Places does support businesses in China.** Microsoft's own guidance
   confirms it, including manual entry of a Chinese province when the dropdown
   does not populate.

All six target markets — Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, the Philippines,
Malaysia and Singapore — are supported by both platforms.

## Where you are now

Almost nowhere, and that is the honest starting point. A search for the brand
returns only your own pages. The only third-party references that exist are two
automated Hong Kong company-registry mirrors:

| Source | What it is | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| crhk.guru | Automated HK Companies Registry mirror | Low — auto-generated, no editorial value |
| hkaddresses.com | Same public data, different aggregator | Low |

Neither is a link worth pursuing. But they matter for one reason: **they show a
Hong Kong registered address, while the website states Chancheng, Foshan.** That
is a NAP conflict, and if you take the Hong Kong GBP route it becomes material.

## The NAP block — use this everywhere, identically

Inconsistency between listings is the most common reason local signals fail to
compound. Fix one form of each and never vary it.

```
Wonder Sourcing
Chancheng District, Foshan, Guangdong, China
+86 189 4131 5597
hello@wondersourcing.com
https://www.wondersourcing.com/
Mon–Sat, 09:00–18:00 (GMT+8)
```

The site and its structured data already carry this — the Organization schema
now includes `telephone` on all 59 content pages. When you list anywhere else,
copy this block rather than retyping it.

**Decide one thing before you start:** whether you will publish a street address.
Google and Bing both require a verifiable location, and a service-area business
can hide the address publicly but still must verify it. If you would rather not
publish the Foshan street address, the Hong Kong entity route is the cleaner fit.

## Priority order

Do these in sequence. The first two have the largest effect on brand search.

### 1. Bing Places (China) — highest value available right now

The only mainstream map/local platform you can list on from Foshan. It feeds Bing
search, Windows and Copilot results.

- Go to bingplaces.com → add a business
- Set **country to China first**; the province field then accepts manual entry
  (type `Guangdong` in pinyin)
- Category: closest match to furniture sourcing / import-export services
- Use the NAP block above verbatim
- Add the description from the copy section below
- Verify — postcard, phone or email depending on address type

### 2. Google Business Profile via the Hong Kong entity

- Create at business.google.com with **Hong Kong** as the country
- Use the registered name `Wonder Sourcing Limited`
- Category candidates to check in the interface (Google's list changes): sourcing
  agent / import-export company / furniture wholesaler / logistics service
- If you operate as a service-area business rather than a storefront, set the
  service area to the six target markets
- Description: use the GBP copy below (750 character limit)
- Verification is normally postcard or video; an overseas postcard is slow, so
  request video verification

> If the HK address is a secretarial or registered-office address, check Google's
> guidelines before listing it. Listings at addresses that cannot accept business
> contact breach policy, and a suspended profile is worse than none.

### 3. Apple Business Connect

Covers Apple Maps, which has meaningful share on iPhone in Southeast Asia. Same
NAP block, free, low effort once the copy exists.

### 4. LinkedIn company page

Not a link play — a credibility signal. Buyers in this sector check LinkedIn
before replying to an unknown supplier. Complete the profile, link the site, post
occasionally.

### 5. Facebook Page

Southeast Asia is Facebook-heavy in a way Europe and North America are not. For
your buyer profile this is arguably more useful than the platform you are already
active on.

### 6. YouTube channel

Factory walkthroughs, QC inspections, loading a container. Video is the closest
substitute for a plant visit, answers the "are you really there" question, and
earns links that text pages do not.

### 7. HKTDC sourcing platform

Given the Hong Kong registration, listing as a service provider on HKTDC's trade
platform is a genuine fit rather than a generic directory entry.

## Linkable assets — the part that earns real links

Directories give citations; to earn links you need something a journalist, a
blogger or a forum poster actively wants to reference. You are unusually well
placed to build these, because you have data nobody outside Foshan has.

Ranked by likely return:

1. **Landed-cost calculator (FOB → delivered)** — a working tool taking FOB
   price, volume, destination and duty rate, returning a delivered cost per unit.
   No equivalent exists for the China→SE Asia furniture lane. Tools attract links
   passively for years.
2. **Port and transit reference for six markets** — you already publish transit
   times; a properly structured, citable reference with ports, operators and
   free-time norms becomes the page people link to instead of a forum answer.
3. **Furniture defect photo glossary** — what pre-shipment inspection actually
   catches, with photographs. Linkable because it is genuinely hard to find.
4. **Chinese New Year shutdown planner** — dates, planning windows, what to order
   when. Seasonal and shareable.
5. **HS code guide for furniture imports into Southeast Asia** — the practical
   detail buyers struggle with most.

Each works on the same principle: publish the thing your buyers currently have
to email you to ask.

## Outreach, honestly

**What works:** a genuinely useful asset, then telling people who already write
about the subject — trade publications, furniture and hospitality procurement
blogs, importer forums, and your own clients (a supplier mention in a case study
is a legitimate citation).

**What to avoid, without exception:**

- Paid "100 backlinks" packages, PBNs and link farms — the clearest link-scheme
  signal Google penalises
- Mass-submitting to generic directories with no editorial oversight
- Exact-match anchor text bought in guest posts
- Reciprocal link exchanges

A profile that grows slowly from things people actually reference will
outperform a large artificial one, and it will not put the domain at risk.

## Ready-to-paste copy

Four lengths, consistent with the site and using only claims already published
there. Use the same text across platforms so the entity reads consistently.

**Tagline (under 10 words)**
> Furniture sourcing, factory auditing and export from Foshan, China.

**Micro — 25 words**
> Wonder Sourcing is a Foshan-based furniture sourcing agency auditing factories,
inspecting production and consolidating containers for importers across Southeast Asia.

**Short — 50 words**
> Wonder Sourcing is a furniture sourcing and export agency based in Foshan,
Guangdong — the world's largest furniture manufacturing cluster. We vet factories,
run three-stage quality inspections, consolidate orders from multiple suppliers and
ship to Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia and Singapore.
English, Vietnamese, Thai and Bahasa spoken in-office.

**Medium — 100 words**
> Wonder Sourcing is a furniture sourcing and export agency based in Chancheng,
Foshan, in Guangdong province — the largest concentration of furniture factories in
the world. We act for importers across Southeast Asia, sitting between your
specification and the factories of Shunde and Lecong: shortlisting and auditing
suppliers, negotiating FOB pricing directly, running independent pre-production,
mid-line and pre-shipment inspections, and consolidating orders from several
manufacturers into a single shipment. We handle export documentation and freight to
six markets, and speak English, Vietnamese, Thai and Bahasa in-office. 240+ factories
audited and more than 1,100 containers shipped a year.

**GBP / long — under 750 characters**
> Furniture sourcing, factory auditing and export from Foshan, China, for buyers
across Southeast Asia. We shortlist and vet factories across Shunde and Lecong,
negotiate FOB pricing directly, inspect production at three stages using our own QC
staff, and consolidate orders from multiple factories into a single container. Export
customs, ocean freight and documentation handled in-house. Markets served: Vietnam,
Thailand, Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia and Singapore. English, Vietnamese,
Thai and Bahasa spoken. No minimum order for factory audits.
